# Cold outreach discovery

Read after the user requests or accepts cold outreach. Apply the candidate's
existing target, hard filters, blacklist, language, and canonical career-ops
data contract. Ask only for missing details that materially change targeting,
such as openness to a small startup, contract work, or a paid pilot. Do not
assume the user accepts unpaid work or freelance engagements.

## Research two kinds of opportunity

1. **Evidence of receptiveness.** Search public founder, hiring-manager, and
   employee posts on LinkedIn, X, public community threads, company blogs, and
   interviews for first-person accounts of getting hired through cold email,
   unsolicited applications, or a direct portfolio approach. Also look for
   explicit invitations to email about working together.
2. **Relevant company needs.** Find active startups, recently launched products,
   and growing businesses whose visible work connects to the candidate's actual
   skills. Start with official product/changelog pages, founder updates, and
   accelerator directories; use public social posts as leads. Funding, a launch,
   or founder activity alone does not establish hiring budget or intent.

Use English and Indonesian search variants, adapted to the role and geography:

- `"hired" "cold email" founder`, `"got hired" "cold email" engineer`
- `"we hired" "cold outreach"`, `"join us" "email me" startup`
- `"diterima kerja" "cold email"`, `"magang" "email founder"`
- `[company] founder hiring`, `[company] careers spontaneous application`
- `[sector] startup Indonesia launch`, `[role] startup APAC hiring`

Open the original source; search snippets, copied threads, and AI summaries
are discovery aids, not verification. Record the event/post date separately
from the access date. Seek recent company activity, ordinarily within 90 days;
historical cold-email stories may be older and must retain their dates. The
seven-day vacancy freshness default does not invalidate historical evidence.
When a social post is inaccessible, report it as unverified and seek an
accessible first-party account. Never invent its contents or bypass access controls.

## Label the evidence precisely

| Label | What the source supports |
| --- | --- |
| Reported hire via cold email | An identifiable employer or candidate explicitly describes a hiring outcome caused by unsolicited email; attribute self-reported claims. |
| Outreach explicitly invited | A company or relevant decision-maker invites career inquiries; this does not prove anyone was hired this way. |
| Speculative fit | Public company activity suggests a relevant problem; openness to unsolicited candidates and an available role remain unconfirmed. |

Distinguish employment/internship outcomes from sales, investment, networking,
and freelance wins. Do not generalize one person's story into a company hiring
policy or a numerical success probability. Prefer a strong candidate fit over
a famous cold-email anecdote at an unsuitable employer.

An advertised job asking applicants to email a resume is an invited email
application, not a cold-email success story. Route it through the normal
application workflow and follow the stated instructions.

## Qualify and rank

Verify that the business is active, identify its product and customers, and
check geography, work model, candidate level, and any public hiring policy.
Use official sources to validate social claims where possible. For speculative
targets, mark compensation, vacancy, and cross-border eligibility as unknown
when unconfirmed; do not present them as passing those filters. Exclude known
incompatibilities and respect instructions to apply only through a stated channel.

For each promising company, connect:

- One observed company activity or problem, linked to its source.
- One real candidate project, skill, or result relevant to that activity.
- A modest contribution hypothesis and a concrete reason to approach now.
- The appropriate person or team and their publicly offered professional
  contact route, with source; mark missing contact routes as unresolved.

Do not infer private email addresses from naming patterns, buy contact lists,
or treat a customer-support address as a hiring contact without evidence. Keep
private contact details out of reports; show public business contacts only
where needed to prepare the requested outreach.

Deduplicate against tracker, pipeline, scan history, and previous outreach,
including company/contact overlap. Respect declines and opt-outs. Apply existing
career-ops scoring where supported; if it requires a JD, leave it unevaluated
and explain fit qualitatively. Never fabricate a JD or score for a speculative
lead. Return up to ten credible targets, with the best five prioritized; fewer
is fine. Separate verified vacancies from unadvertised opportunity hypotheses.

For each target, report company, proposed role/contribution, fit and why now,
evidence label, source links and dates, contact route, material unknowns, and
the recommended next step. A compact table plus short notes is enough. If no
verified cold-email hiring stories are found, say so and keep any useful
invited/speculative leads correctly labeled.

## Prepare the approach

Use canonical outreach/email modes for drafts. Start with the strongest one
to three targets unless the user requests more. Each draft should have a clear
subject, one verified company-specific observation, relevant candidate proof,
a bounded contribution idea, and one easy next step. Aim for roughly 80–150
words; adapt the language and tone to the recipient and user preference.
Avoid generic praise, fabricated familiarity, inflated achievements, and
promises of unpaid custom work. Link only the relevant approved portfolio item.

Research and draft autonomously within the accepted scope. Before sending,
ensure the recipient, message, and attachments fall within explicit user
authorization; if authorization is missing, prepare these for review first.
An accepted cold-email research offer is not permission to contact anyone.

Save only through existing supported reports/tracker fields when the requested
mode calls for it. Keep unsent drafts distinct from contacted or applied
records; preserve evidence labels and uncertainties in supported notes rather
than inventing tracker columns or a separate CRM. A draft is never a sent email.
Suggest one brief follow-up after about 5–7 business days when appropriate;
do not send or schedule it without authorization. Stop on a refusal or opt-out.
